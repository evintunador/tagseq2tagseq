# liu2024lostmiddle — Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts

Liu, Lin, Hewitt, Paranjape, Bevilacqua, Petroni, Liang. TACL vol. 12, pp. 157–173 (2024). arXiv 2307.03172.
Sources for this note: arXiv abstract + full HTML (ar5iv). Numbers below are taken from the paper; where I could not pin an exact figure I say "curve-level" rather than invent one.

## What the paper actually does

A controlled analysis (not a new model) of *where* in a long input relevant information must sit for an LLM to use it. Two synthetic-but-realistic probes, both of which let the authors move the relevant content to any position while holding everything else fixed:

**1. Multi-document QA.** A question plus k retrieved Wikipedia documents (from NaturalQuestions-Open), exactly one of which contains the answer; the other k−1 are topically-related distractors returned by a retriever. Two knobs are varied independently: (a) *number of documents* k ∈ {10, 20, 30} (controls total input length), and (b) the *index position* of the gold document within the list (0 = first … k−1 = last). Accuracy is measured as a function of the gold position.

**2. Key–value retrieval.** A synthetic JSON object of {UUID-key: UUID-value} pairs; the model must return the value for one queried key. Zero semantics, pure "find the token span at position p." Lengths of 75, 140, 300 pairs, 500 examples each. Isolates the positional-access ability from any reasoning.

**Models (all evaluated, not trained):** decoder-only closed — GPT-3.5-Turbo (4K) and its 16K variant, Claude-1.3 (8K) and Claude-1.3-100K, GPT-4 (8K); decoder-only open — MPT-30B-Instruct (8192-ctx, ALiBi positions), LongChat-13B (16K), Llama-2 7B/13B/70B; encoder-decoder — Flan-T5-XXL (trained ≤512), Flan-UL2 (trained ≤2048).

**Headline result — the U-shaped curve.** Accuracy is highest when the gold document is at the *very beginning* (primacy) or *very end* (recency) of the context and sags in the *middle*. Concrete multi-doc QA numbers (GPT-3.5-Turbo):
- 10 docs: pos 0 = 76.8%, middle (pos 4) = 61.2%, last (pos 9) = 62.4%.
- 20 docs: pos 0 = 75.8%, middle (pos 9) = 53.8%, last (pos 19) = 63.2%.
- 30 docs (16K variant): pos 0 = 73.4%, middle = 50.5%, last (pos 29) = 63.7%.
The best-minus-worst gap is ~20 points, and it *widens with more documents / longer context*. Strikingly, GPT-3.5-Turbo's worst-position (middle) accuracy can fall *below its closed-book* score (56.1%) — i.e., a correctly-retrieved gold document placed mid-context hurts less than useless. Closed-book vs. oracle-single-doc bounds (Table 1): GPT-3.5-Turbo 56.1 / 88.3; Claude-1.3 48.3 / 76.1; LongChat-13B 35.0 / 83.4; MPT-30B-Instruct 31.5 / 81.9. Claude-1.3's curve is flatter (pos 0 = 59.9, pos 4 = 55.9, pos 19 = 60.1) but still U-shaped.

**Key–value retrieval.** Claude-1.3 and Claude-1.3-100K are ~perfect at all lengths; the rest degrade at 140/300 pairs with the same U-shape (worst-case ~45.6% without the query-repeat trick). Shows the effect is not about reasoning — it is positional token access.

**Extended-context ≠ better use of context.** When an input fits both a base and its long-context sibling (GPT-3.5-Turbo vs -16K; Claude-1.3 vs -100K), the two curves are "nearly superimposed." A bigger context window does not make the model use the middle better.

**Query-aware contextualization.** Putting the query *both before and after* the documents/pairs: dramatically fixes key–value retrieval (models reach ~perfect, incl. GPT-3.5-Turbo-16K at 300 pairs) but *barely moves* the multi-doc QA U-curve (small gain only when gold is already near the start). So repeating the query lets a decoder-only model "look back" for a literal key match but does not repair semantic use of buried evidence.

**Architecture.** Encoder-decoder models (Flan-T5, Flan-UL2) are *relatively position-robust within their training length* (Flan-UL2 best-vs-worst gap only 1.9% within 2048 tokens) but develop the U-curve once inputs exceed training length. The authors hypothesize the bidirectional encoder lets every document be contextualized against all others, so importance estimation is position-independent — a decoder-only model, seeing documents strictly left-to-right under causal masking, cannot. Instruction tuning raises absolute accuracy and shrinks (not removes) the worst-case gap (MPT-30B ~10% → MPT-30B-Instruct ~4%). Model scale matters: Llama-2-7B is *only recency-biased* (no primacy bump); the U-shape (both biases) emerges at 13B/70B.

**Retriever-reader practical upshot.** On open NaturalQuestions with a Contriever retriever, reader accuracy *saturates long before retriever recall does*: 20→50 documents buys only ~1.5% (GPT-3.5-Turbo) / ~1% (Claude-1.3). More retrieved context is nearly wasted; they suggest reranking so the best passage sits at an extremity, and truncating the list.

## Methodology: theirs vs. ours

The paper is a **diagnosis**; ours is a **prescription** aimed squarely at that diagnosis. The axis:

- **Their intervention = none (observational).** They move the gold doc and watch a *frozen* model. No training-on-structure, no attention change. Our project's whole thesis is an *architectural + training-time* intervention: a custom sparse attention mask (`cross_doc_link` in `model/graph_traversal/cross_doc_mask.py`) grants a linking document a direct attention edge into the doc it links, from the link position onward (grant rect rows `[link_end_pos, A.end)` × cols `[B.start, B.end)`, per masks.md §Formal semantics). The neighbor is not merely *placed* somewhere in the context and hoped-for — it is *wired* to the linker.
- **Where the effect hits.** Lost-in-the-middle predicts a buried mid-context neighbor is under-used. Two of our design decisions directly evade the middle:
  1. **Packing order** (`prefer_targets_first`, pack_sampler.py:476-558 per packing_density.md): Kahn topo sort places linked-*to* documents *before* linkers and emits each connected component as a contiguous block. So the gold neighbor is (a) topologically upstream and (b) locally adjacent to the linker, not scattered.
  2. **Inference retrieval by insertion** (generation_retrieval.md, `_handle_link` generation_loop.py:216-381): when a generated link fires, the fetched target is inserted *immediately before the linking doc* (`_docs.insert(idx)`, `before_entry=active_entry`) — literally adjacent — and then the `cross_doc_link` mask grants the linker attention to it. This is the exact opposite of the failure mode: rather than dropping a retrieved doc into a long prompt and hoping the model reads it (the retriever-reader setting the paper shows saturates), we place it adjacent *and* give it a dedicated attention edge.
- **Attention edge vs. the alternatives.** Our edge is a *masked full-attention* connection inside a single forward pass — not a GNN message-passing edge, not a cached-KV reuse, not merely a training-pair co-occurrence signal. It is closest in spirit to "let the decoder attend across the document boundary," which is precisely the capability the paper argues *bidirectional encoders* have and decoder-only models lack. In effect we are trying to give a decoder-only LM some of the cross-document contextualization the paper credits to encoder-decoders, but selectively (only along graph edges) rather than globally.
- **What we share.** Both care about *decoder-only* models over multi-document contexts; both treat "relevant info is somewhere in a long input" as the core problem. Their key–value probe and our link-following are the same operation abstractly: locate the right span elsewhere in the sequence and use it.
- **Where we diverge hardest.** They keep positional structure fixed and vary content position; we keep the *edge* fixed and let absolute positions float. Our masks.md flags that **RoPE is not reset per document** (attention.py, reviewer-attackable #1): a linker at global position i reads a target at relative offset (i − target_pos) = the *packing distance*, which is traversal-order-dependent and can be large/arbitrary. This paper is the strongest external evidence that *relative position within a long context materially changes utilization* — so our no-reset choice is not positionally neutral, and their U-curve is the mechanism by which it could bite.

## Predictions & open questions for our method

1. **Adjacency + edge should recover most of the ~20-point middle penalty.** Because inference retrieval inserts the target adjacent to the linker and packing keeps components contiguous, the "relevant doc" is at a *local extremity* relative to the query position, not the global middle. Prediction: our cross-doc-link condition should show far less sensitivity to *where in the 32k pack* a component sits than a naive concat baseline that leaves the neighbor at its retrieved rank. This is a clean ablation: sweep the component's absolute offset in the pack and measure link-utilization — the edge should flatten the curve the way the encoder-decoder does in their Fig.
2. **The compute-control masks predict a *positive* linking effect beyond FLOPs.** `doc_concatenated` / `doc_concat_link` (masks.md) give the same or more attention connectivity without the *link-position gating*. If lost-in-the-middle is the mechanism, then `cross_doc_link` should beat plain `doc_concatenated` *specifically when the neighbor would otherwise be mid-context*, because the direct edge short-circuits the positional decay. Where the neighbor is already adjacent, the gap should shrink — a testable interaction.
3. **RoPE-position implication (the central worry).** The paper shows utilization depends on position, and our RoPE is not reset per doc, so a target reached at a large packing distance sits at a large *relative* RoPE offset from the linker. Two compounding risks: (a) their recency/primacy structure suggests mid-distance offsets are the weakest — so a component packed such that the target lands "in the middle" of the linker's attended span is the worst case; (b) chen2023positioninterpolation (already in refs) warns that large *untrained* relative RoPE offsets destabilize. Prediction: link-following quality should *degrade with packing distance* between linker and target, and this degradation is partly the lost-in-the-middle curve expressed in RoPE-offset space. Worth logging link-utilization vs. (link_pos − target_pos). If severe, per-doc position reset or distance-capped packing is the mitigation; kazemnejad2023positional / ruoss2023randomized (already cited) are the counter-argument that positions are interchangeable under enough variety.
4. **Query-aware contextualization has a partial analogue.** Their result that *repeating the query around the data* fixes key–value but not semantic QA suggests our edge (a structural, not textual, "pointer") is the right tool for the semantic case where query-repetition failed. Our advantage over their retriever-reader baseline: we do not rely on the model spontaneously scanning a long list — the mask forces the read.
5. **Scale caveat.** The U-shape (both biases) only emerged at ≥13B in Llama-2; 7B was recency-only. If our pretraining runs are small, we may see *recency-dominated* rather than U-shaped behavior, which changes the optimal packing order (targets-first assumes primacy helps; a recency-only regime would argue for targets *after* or immediately-adjacent — which our inference insertion already does).
6. **Open question our design could resolve for them.** They ask whether models can be *made* to use the middle. Our `cross_doc_link` vs. `doc_concatenated` compute-control is a direct experiment: does a *trained-in* direct edge give a decoder-only model position-robust cross-document use (their encoder-decoder property) without going bidirectional? A positive result answers their architectural hypothesis constructively.

## Gotchas

- **The distractors are non-trivial.** Their distractors are *retriever-returned, topically similar* docs, not random text — adding random distractors even *raised* absolute accuracy while keeping the U-shape. Our multi-doc / HotpotQA-style eval (related_work_notes.md) should use hard, on-topic distractors or we will overstate the linking win.
- **Middle < closed-book.** A correct-but-buried neighbor can be *worse than no neighbor*. If our concat baselines leave the target mid-context, they may underperform a no-context baseline — do not misread that as "linking is unnecessary"; it is the failure mode we are fixing. Report closed-book/no-edge bounds like their Table 1.
- **Extended-context window ≠ utilization.** Do not claim our 8k→32k RoPE-inference (generation_retrieval.md: length-agnostic, pure RoPE) *by itself* improves long-context use — they show a bigger window alone does nothing. Our claim must rest on the edge + adjacency, not the window size.
- **max_grants truncation is a positional bias.** masks.md #4: grants beyond the cap (256 in production) are dropped *sorted by link_end_pos* — later links lose their grant first. That is a self-inflicted recency/primacy artifact layered on top of the model's own — if a pack has >256 links, the paper's exact failure (positionally-biased access) is reintroduced by our own kernel. Flag and bound.
- **Query-aware trick won't save semantic tasks.** If we add query-repetition as a baseline, expect it to help only literal-match probes, not real multi-hop QA — matches their finding; don't tune on the easy case.
- **Analytical density proxy is position-agnostic.** packing_density.md #6: the kv_block_count metric counts grant *rectangles*, not their absolute position; two packs with identical density can have the target at very different offsets. Density bucketing therefore does *not* control for lost-in-the-middle exposure — a confound if we ever correlate loss with density.

## caciularu2021cdlm — CDLM: Cross-Document Language Modeling

*(Caciularu, Cohan, Beltagy, Peters, Cattan, Dagan — Findings of EMNLP 2021, arXiv:2101.00406v2. All numbers below read off the paper PDF; ablation/hyperparameter details are from its appendices.)*

### What the paper actually does

**One line.** Continue-pretrain an *encoder* Longformer over **clusters of related documents** (not isolated docs), with a **dynamic global-attention pattern placed on the masked tokens**, so the MLM objective is forced to "peek" across documents. Two orthogonal ideas — related-document context and pretraining-time global attention — that the paper argues work *in synergy*.

**Base model & scale.** Longformer-base (the encoder-only long-doc transformer: sliding-window *local* attention + a few *global* tokens, with **separate weight matrices** for local vs. global). Context length **4,096 tokens**. They **continue** HuggingFace Longformer-base pretraining for only **25k additional steps** (seq len 4096, effective batch 64, lr 3e-5, 500-step warmup, power-3 poly decay, fp16). 8 days on 8×48GB RTX8000. They explicitly did *not* train a large version (cited ~3× compute).

**Pretraining data — the key structural point.** Source = the **Multi-News** multi-document-summarization corpus (Fabbri 2019): 44,972 training clusters, each a set of 2–10 news articles about the *same story*. They keep clusters with **≥3 documents** (21,078 clusters per Table 7), concatenate randomly-sampled docs from a cluster with special separators **⟨doc-s⟩ … ⟨/doc-s⟩** until they fill 4,096 tokens. The corpus is tiny by LM standards (322MB uncompressed). "Relatedness" is *topical* (same news event), given by the pre-existing Multi-News clustering — **not** an explicit per-link edge and **not** a citation/hyperlink graph.

**The mechanism ("dynamic global attention").** Standard BERT-style MLM (15% masked). The novelty: at pretraining time they assign **global attention to exactly the masked token positions** (dynamic = it moves with the mask each example), so a masked token attends to the *entire* concatenated cluster (all docs), using the global-attention weight matrices; non-masked tokens keep local (windowed) attention. This is the fix to Longformer's limitation that **global-attention weights are never pretrained** — Longformer initializes them from the local weights only at fine-tune time. CDLM pretrains them.

**Results (concrete numbers).**
- **CD perplexity** (Multi-News test, mask-and-predict across docs): Longformer 3.94 → Local-CDLM 3.84 → Rand-CDLM 3.81 → **CDLM 3.39** / Prefix-CDLM 3.41. Global-attention variants dominate the local ones.
- **ECB+ cross-document coreference** (CoNLL F1): **events 85.6** (prev SOTA Yu 2020 = 84.4; Zeng 2020 = 84.3), **entities 82.9** (prev SOTA Allaway 2021 = 75.3 → +7.6). Beats baselines that use *large* models + external linguistic features, using a base model. Ablation (event CoNLL F1): full-doc CDLM 85.6 vs. sentences-only 84.2 (−1.4), vs. Longformer 84.6 (−1.0), Local-CDLM 84.7 (−0.9), Rand-CDLM 84.1 (−1.5), Prefix-CDLM 85.1 (−0.5).
- **Document matching** (F1): citation-recommendation AAN **88.8**, OC **95.3**, S2ORC **96.5** — all beating task-specific CDA. Plagiarism **PAN 82.9** — here CDLM does *not* win (Longformer 80.4, Prefix 81.7; BERT-HAN+CDA 86.2 / but CDA *hurt* HAN, suggesting PAN needs no cross-doc attention; small dataset).
- **HotpotQA-distractor** (dev, base size): CDLM Joint **66.3** (Ans 74.7 / Sup 86.3). Beats Longformer (64.5), all CDLM ablations, and comparably-sized prior work — **but loses to BigBird (67.8)**, which was pretrained on vastly more data/compute. Gains concentrate in **supporting-fact (evidence) detection**, not answer span.
- **Attention probe** (no fine-tuning): global-attention models give co-referring target spans a higher mean percentile rank (positive events: Longformer 61.9 → CDLM 72.1; entities 59.7 → 70.3), while negatives stay near chance — evidence the pretrained global attention *implicitly encodes* cross-doc alignment.

**Baselines that define the ablation grid** (all share corpus/hparams): **Longformer** (no extra pretrain), **Local-CDLM** (same corpus, local-only attention — isolates "just more pretraining"), **Rand-CDLM** (global attention but *unrelated* docs from different clusters — isolates "relatedness"), **Prefix-CDLM** (global attention on the *first* tokens, à la BigBird — isolates *where* global attention goes). The clean takeaway: relatedness *and* global-on-masked each help, and neither alone recovers full CDLM.

### Methodology: theirs vs. ours

This is flagged as our sharpest *cross-document pretraining* comparator, and it is — but the mechanism differs from ours on almost every axis. Grounding for "ours" is `code_briefs/masks.md`, `packing_density.md`, `traversal.md` (all @ commit 6134163).

| Axis | CDLM | TAGSeq2TAGSeq (ours) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure signal | **Topical cluster** (Multi-News same-story sets); relatedness is a set-membership label, not an edge | **Explicit directed graph edge** (hyperlink / import / citation), detected at a token position (`link_detector.py`, 11 syntaxes) |
| Where structure enters | Which docs get **concatenated** into the 4096 window + which positions get global attention | Which docs get **packed** together (graph traversal, `traversal.py`) **and** a per-link **attention grant** rectangle (`cross_doc_mask.py`) |
| Attention edge | **Symmetric** global attention: masked token ↔ *all* tokens in *all* docs; no direction, no per-pair edge | **Asymmetric, directed, link-gated**: link A→B grants rows `[link_end_pos, A.end)` × cols `[B.start,B.end)` only; A reads B, never the transpose (`masks.md` §Formal semantics) |
| Objective / arch | **Encoder MLM**, bidirectional, BERT-style 15% mask | **Decoder-only causal LM**; causal `q>=k` never relaxed; the link grant rides *on top of* causality |
| Granularity of "peek" | Whole-cluster: every masked token sees everything (dense global rows) | Point-to-rectangle: a *specific* link position reads a *specific* target doc's token span, bit-packed grants (`masks.md` §bit-packed) |
| Train vs. inference symmetry | Global-on-masked at pretrain; at fine-tune, global moved to task tokens ([CLS], ⟨m⟩, mentions, titles) — **the attention pattern changes between phases** | **Identical mask at pretrain and generation**; a generated link deterministically fetches its target into the attention context (Option B baked grants for train/eval, text-detection at generation — `masks.md` §Option B) |
| Compute control | Rand-CDLM / Local-CDLM / Prefix-CDLM isolate *relatedness* and *global-attention placement* | `doc_concat_link` / `doc_concatenated` isolate the *link-position gating* from raw FLOPs — strict superset ordering `doc_causal < cross_doc_link ≤ doc_concat_link ≤ doc_concatenated` (`masks.md`) |
| Position handling | Longformer absolute positions within 4096; no cross-doc position issue at their scale | Global RoPE positions [0,T) over a 32k pack, **no per-doc reset**; A reads B at packing-distance offset (`masks.md` reviewer-attackable #1) |
| Scale | 4,096 ctx, 25k continue-pretrain steps, base encoder | 32k ctx, decoder trained with custom Triton block-sparse kernels |

**The single sharpest contrast.** CDLM's "cross-document reading" is **symmetric, global, and cluster-scoped**: a masked token bidirectionally attends to *everything* in the window, and *which* documents co-occur is chosen by an external topical clustering. Ours is **directed, sparse, and edge-scoped**: the graph edge is the compute primitive — a link position gets read-access to *one named target*, in the causal direction only, and the same edge fires identically at generation. CDLM proves that *co-locating related documents + letting the objective attend across them* already helps a lot; we further claim the *specific link edge* (not just co-location) carries signal, which is exactly what our `doc_concatenated` compute-control is designed to test — that control is the analog of their Rand-vs-CDLM / Local-vs-CDLM grid, but isolating *link gating* rather than *relatedness/placement*.

**Where we converge.** (1) Both pretrain *on structure* rather than retrieving at inference — CDLM is squarely on our side of the train-on-structure axis, unlike RAG/kNN-LM/Memorizing-Transformer. (2) Both use an **attention edge**, not a GNN message-passing edge, not a cached-KV store, not a contrastive training-pair label — this is what makes CDLM the closest mechanistic cousin among the graph-pretraining antecedents (`related_work_notes.md` §"Pretraining directly on link structure"). (3) Both use **special separator tokens** to mark document boundaries in a concatenated sequence (their ⟨doc-s⟩ vs. our layout prefix cards + EOS, `packing_density.md` §Pack layout). (4) Both build compute-matched ablations to attribute gains to structure rather than extra data/FLOPs.

### Predictions & open questions for our method

1. **Structure helps most on genuinely cross-document tasks; single-doc controls stay flat.** CDLM's gains are large on ECB+ coref and citation-matching (inherently cross-text) and *vanish* on PAN plagiarism (their words: "does not require detailed cross-document attention at all"). Prediction: our cross-doc edge should move HotpotQA/2WikiMultiHop and citation/link-completion metrics, and should be a *no-op* on the single-document MC controls we already earmarked (HellaSwag, ARC, PIQA in `related_work_notes.md` §Single-document controls). If our edge moves the single-doc controls, that's a leakage/artifact signal.

2. **The effect concentrates in evidence-finding, not answer generation.** On HotpotQA the CDLM lift was in **supporting-fact detection** (Sup 83.9→86.3) far more than answer span (Ans 74.5→74.7). Prediction for us: the link edge should most improve *locating/attending to the right target doc* — measurable as Δnll on tokens whose prediction depends on the linked doc — more than uniformly lowering loss. Design our paired-Δnll eval to bucket by "token needs cross-doc info" vs. not.

3. **A tiny amount of structured continue-pretraining goes a long way.** 25k steps on 322MB flipped multiple SOTAs. This supports our compute-control framing: if `cross_doc_link` beats `doc_concatenated` at *matched* FLOPs, the win is the gating, not the tokens — CDLM's Rand-CDLM (matched compute, unrelated docs, *still* underperforms CDLM) is direct precedent that *what you attend to* matters beyond *how much you attend*.

4. **Placement of the attention privilege matters — our DAG gate is the risky knob.** Prefix-CDLM (global on first tokens, BigBird-style) nearly matched CDLM on perplexity but trailed on coref, i.e. *where* the cross-doc attention is allowed changes downstream behavior even at equal FLOPs. Our analog is the **backward-links-only DAG gate** + `prefer_targets_first` topo ordering (`traversal.md` §Mask interaction): if targets don't precede linkers, grants are *silently dropped* and we degenerate toward `doc_causal`. Predict our results are highly sensitive to realized-vs-raw link density; quantify the drop rate (their Prefix/CDLM near-tie warns that a *misplaced* edge can look fine on perplexity while losing the real downstream effect).

5. **Symmetric vs. causal-directed edge is an open empirical question CDLM leaves us.** They needed *bidirectional* global attention (encoder). We bet a *causal, directional* edge suffices for generation. CDLM's success with symmetry raises the question: does our directional restriction lose the "align suing↔alleges" bidirectional matching signal? Our resolution — the target is always packed *before* the linker (topo order), so the causal reader still sees the full target — is precisely the design point that lets a directed causal mask recover a bidirectional-flavored benefit. Worth stating explicitly as our answer to their open bidirectionality assumption.

6. **Our design resolves their scale/generation limitation.** CDLM explicitly punts on (a) larger models and (b) seq2seq/generative tasks (encoder-only). Our decoder-only generative setup with a fetch-on-generated-link mechanism is exactly the "cross-document seq2seq" future work they call for — a clean framing: we generalize CDLM's cross-doc *encoding* to cross-doc *generation*.

### Gotchas

- **Cluster quality dominates.** CDLM's whole signal comes from Multi-News's topical clustering; Rand-CDLM (random docs) erases the benefit. For us the analog failure is **traversal producing incoherent packs** (uniform-seeded walks, frontier restarts pulling unrelated repos — `traversal.md` §Budget/Determinism). If our packs are effectively random-related, we become Rand-CDLM. Audit realized topical/graph coherence of packs before trusting a null result.
- **Small, specialized pretraining corpus = domain-narrow gains + contamination risk.** 322MB of news. Their downstream wins are on news-adjacent/scientific-abstract tasks. Don't over-generalize a corpus-shaped result; and note ECB+/HotpotQA are old (2014/2018) Wikipedia/news — the same **HotpotQA-2017 leakage** concern already flagged in our notes applies to any cross-doc benchmark we borrow.
- **The pattern changes between pretrain and fine-tune — a confound they live with.** CDLM pretrains global-on-masked but fine-tunes global-on-task-tokens ([CLS], mention, title, sentence markers). That train/test attention mismatch is a known Longformer wart (global weights merely *initialized*, then adapted). Our stated advantage is an **identical mask train↔inference**; make sure it *really is* identical — `masks.md` flags that Flex (inference) vs. Triton (train) vs. dense-viz are *three* mask reimplementations that must agree, and that **max_grants must match** train/eval or we understate the effect. This is the CDLM confound reappearing as an implementation-parity bug.
- **Perplexity is a weak proxy for the real effect.** Prefix-CDLM ≈ CDLM on perplexity (3.41 vs 3.39) but Prefix lost on coref. Don't gate our claims on LM loss alone; use the downstream/paired-Δnll cross-doc metric. (Matches our `biderman2024lessons`/`schaeffer2023mirage` metric-artifact discipline.)
- **A cross-doc mechanism can *hurt* when the task doesn't need it** (PAN; and CDA *degraded* BERT-HAN there). Expect our edge to be neutral-to-harmful on tasks with no genuine cross-doc dependency; keep those as controls, don't average them into a headline.
- **Statistical rigor bar.** They report bootstrap + permutation tests at p<0.001. Our bootstrap-CI-excludes-zero + paired + derangement-placebo gate is the right analog; hold it.

### Missed citations worth adding

Scanned CDLM's own reference list against `paper/bib/refs.bib` (grep-verified missing). Genuinely absent and relevant to us:

- **zhou2020crossdocattention** — Zhou, Pappas, Smith, "Multilevel Text Alignment with Cross-Document Attention," EMNLP 2020, **arXiv:2010.01263**. The CDA method: hierarchical cross-document *attention* between a document pair for matching (citation-rec / plagiarism). Directly relevant — it is a *cross-document attention* mechanism (not pretraining) and the source of CDLM's document-matching benchmarks (AAN/OC/S2ORC/PAN); a clean "task-specific cross-doc attention vs. our pretrained edge" contrast, and the AAN/S2ORC citation-graph tasks overlap our citation corpus.
- **asai2020reasoningpaths** — Asai, Hashimoto, Hajishirzi, Socher, Xiong, "Learning to Retrieve Reasoning Paths over Wikipedia Graph for Question Answering," ICLR 2020, **arXiv:1911.10470**. Graph Recurrent Retriever: retrieves *reasoning paths over the Wikipedia link graph* for multi-hop QA — the retrieve-at-inference-over-a-hyperlink-graph counterpart to our train-on-the-graph approach; strong foil for the HotpotQA setting. (Note: our refs contain three unrelated "Asai" entries; this one is not present.)
- **zhao2020transformerxh** — Zhao, Xiong, Rosset, Song, Bennett, Tiwary, "Transformer-XH: Multi-Evidence Reasoning with Extra Hop Attention," ICLR 2020 (OpenReview; no arXiv id found — verify venue string, do not invent an eprint). Adds "extra hop" attention edges across evidence nodes for multi-hop QA — an *attention-edge-across-documents* method, mechanistically close to ours but bolted on at task time rather than pretrained. Good direct comparator.
- **conneau2019xlm** — Conneau & Lample, "Cross-lingual Language Model Pretraining," NeurIPS 2019, **arXiv:1901.07291**. Their TLM objective concatenates a sentence and its translation and masks across both — the canonical "pretrain by masking across two aligned texts" ancestor CDLM cites as conceptual kin. Relevant to us as the earliest *cross-text MLM via concatenation* precedent. (Our refs have `lample2019productkey`, a *different* Lample paper — XLM is absent.)
- **ginzburg2021dcs** — Ginzburg, Malkiel, Barkan, Caciularu, Koenigstein, "Self-Supervised Document Similarity Ranking via Contextualized Language Models and Hierarchical Inference" (DCS/SDR), Findings of ACL-IJCNLP 2021, **arXiv:2106.01186**. Unsupervised cross-document *pair* matching via contextualized LMs — a self-supervised cross-doc finetuning scheme; relevant to our document-matching/link-completion evaluation framing.

Lower priority / likely out of scope (flagging, not recommending): Yang 2016 Hierarchical Attention Networks, Yang 2020 SMITH, Jiang 2019 SMASH (document-matching baselines); Cybulska & Vossen 2014 ECB+ and Cattan 2020 streamlining-coref (only if we run CD-coref eval — `cattan2021crossdoc` and `barhom2019crossdoc` are already in refs, but the ECB+ corpus paper and Cattan-2020 modeling paper are not).

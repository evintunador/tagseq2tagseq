## taylor2022galactica — Galactica: A Large Language Model for Science

*(arXiv 2211.09085; Taylor, Kardas, Cucurull, Scialom, Hartshorn, Saravia, Poulton, Kerkez, Stojnic; Meta AI, 2022.)*
Numbers below are confirmed against the paper (ar5iv full text) unless flagged as inferred.

### What the paper actually does

**Core idea.** Galactica is a decoder-only autoregressive LM trained on a *small, curated, high-quality* scientific corpus rather than a large uncurated web scrape — 106B tokens over 48M papers plus reference material (8M), code (2M), knowledge bases (2M), filtered CommonCrawl (0.9M), and 1.3M prompts. Five model sizes: 125M, 1.3B, 6.7B, 30B, 120B. Standard Transformer decoder, **2048-token context** for all sizes (the paper flags this as a limitation). BPE vocab of 50k learned from a 2% subset; digits tokenized individually; modality-specific wrappers (`[START_SMILES]`, `[START_AMINO]`, `[START_DNA]`, `<work>`).

**Citation as a generation task — the part relevant to us.** Galactica represents the "implicit citation graph" directly in the token stream: every in-text citation is wrapped in special `[START_REF]` … `[END_REF]` tokens, with the cited paper named by a **global identifier**. They tested two identifier schemes — alphanumeric IDs vs. paper *titles* — and chose **title-based identifiers** because they gave higher prediction accuracy (at the cost of more hallucination at small scale). Format is title-then-author, e.g. `[START_REF]Long Short-Term Memory, Hochreiter[END_REF]`. Citation prediction is then pure next-token generation: prompt the model with a document context ending in `[START_REF]` and let it generate the reference string.

**Citation-prediction results (exact numbers).** Three eval sets: **PWC Citations** (644 ML-concept→paper pairs from Papers-with-Code), **Extended Citations** (110 non-ML concept pairs, e.g. Kozak sequence, Breit-Wigner), and **Contextual Citations** (1,869 reference/context pairs from the arXiv validation set). Accuracy scales smoothly and strongly with model size:

| Model | PWC | Extended | Contextual |
|-------|-----|----------|------------|
| 125M | 7.0% | 6.4% | 7.1% |
| 1.3B | 18.5% | 45.5% | 15.9% |
| 6.7B | 32.0% | 60.0% | 23.0% |
| 30B | 44.7% | 66.4% | 31.5% |
| 120B | **51.9%** | **69.1%** | **36.6%** |

Retrieval baselines: sparse (ElasticSearch/BM25) 30.9% / 17.3% / 5.3%; dense base Contriever 16.4% / 8.8% / 1.6%; dense Contriever fine-tuned on 10M context/paper pairs (FAISS index) 27.6% / 11.8% / 8.2%. **Galactica's parametric generation beats every retriever on every dataset once it is ≥6.7B**, and the 1.3B model already beats retrievers on Extended and Contextual. The headline claim: at larger scale the LM "outperforms the retrieval-based approaches as its context-associative power improves," suggesting LMs could become a literature-navigation tool.

**Popularity/frequency bias analysis.** They compare the model's predicted-reference frequency distribution to ground truth via Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance on Contextual Citations. Small models over-predict popular/high-citation papers; as scale grows, KS distance drops and the distribution approaches ground truth. But **bias toward popular papers persists even at 120B** — flagged as a reason the model "likely requires augmentation before being used in production."

**Other findings that touch us.** "Repeated Tokens Considered Not Harmful": they train ~4.25 epochs (450B tokens over the 106B corpus) and validation loss *keeps falling* through four epochs, with the 120B only beginning to overfit at the start of epoch five — attributed to a data quality/modality factor. Prompt pre-training (ExT5/Aribandi-style) mixes task prompts into the corpus. A `<work>` working-memory token wraps step-by-step reasoning and can offload to Python. They explicitly endorse retrieval augmentation as complementary future work ("a strong case … even for larger models," especially for fine-grained knowledge).

### Methodology: theirs vs. ours

The axis the brief asks me to nail — **train-on-structure vs. retrieve-at-inference; attention edge vs. GNN vs. cached-KV vs. training-pair signal** — puts Galactica in a sharply defined spot: it is the closest prior to *"a generated link fetches the target,"* yet it deliberately never fetches anything.

**What we share.** Both projects (a) turn the citation/link graph into a *generation target inside the token stream*, marked by special delimiter tokens, and (b) do so at *pretraining* time, not as an inference-only bolt-on. Galactica's `[START_REF]…[END_REF]` is a direct analog of a link span, and its "generate the identifier of the cited work" is exactly the first half of our inference loop. Our generation loop even mirrors its shape: `generation_loop.py` detects a closed link on the trailing tokens (the equivalent of the model emitting a complete `[…END_REF]` payload) and only then acts (`generation_retrieval.md`, `_generate_doc`/`_handle_link`). Both also share the empirical lesson that this *scales* — Galactica's citation accuracy is monotone in model size across two orders of magnitude.

**Where we fundamentally diverge — the load-bearing contrast.** In Galactica the reference string is the *entire* mechanism: the model emits a title, and that title **is the answer**. There is **no retrieval of, and no attention into, the cited document**. Its knowledge of what a paper contains lives entirely in the 30B/120B parameters, memorized from having read the paper's text elsewhere in the corpus during pretraining. Our method does the opposite at the decisive step: when our generation loop closes a link, `_handle_link` resolves the target via the *same detector match key training uses* (`index_doc_span`, `document_corpus.py`) and **physically inserts the target document's verbatim tokens into the packed sequence immediately before the linking doc**, so the next `build_sequence()` + `forward_inference()` lets the linking positions attend into the fetched content through the `cross_doc_link` grant rectangle (`masks.md`: rows `[link_end_pos, A.end)` × cols `[B.start, B.end)`). The edge is a **hard block-sparse attention edge realized on real target tokens**, used *identically* in pretraining (Option-B baked graph-edge grants) and inference (text-detected links) — a single implementation in both regimes (`generation_retrieval.md`, "Train/inference mirror"). Galactica's is a **training-pair / parametric-memorization signal**: the citation graph shapes the loss but is never a read-access channel; nothing about the cited paper is available at generation time except what the weights recall.

Put in the brief's taxonomy: Galactica is **train-on-structure via parametric memorization** (no edge object at all — the "edge" collapses into the emitted string); ours is **train-on-structure via a materialized attention edge** that also fires as **retrieve-by-insertion at inference**. Galactica has no GNN, no cached KV, and — unlike our design — no notion of the target document being present in the context window when the citation is produced. Its 2048 context could not hold cited documents even if it wanted to; our 32k packing exists precisely to host the linked target.

Two secondary contrasts worth stating in the paper:
- **Identifier resolution.** Galactica generates a free-text title and *is* scored on string match; hallucinated titles are simply wrong. We generate a link, then run deterministic 3-tier resolution (exact raw_identifier → detector-key `index_doc_span` → fuzzy `HashNormTitleIndex`) against a real corpus (`generation_retrieval.md`). The generated string is a *fetch key*, not a terminal answer — the same distinction we draw against decao2021genre/tay2022dsi in the lit review, and it applies equally to Galactica.
- **Popularity bias vs. exact lookup.** Galactica's residual popular-paper bias (persisting at 120B) is inherent to parametric recall of a long-tailed citation distribution. Our exact hashmap resolution has no frequency prior at all: a correctly-generated link to a rare node fetches it as reliably as a common one. This is a concrete advantage to claim.

### Predictions & open questions for our method

**For our arXiv (citation) arm specifically — what Galactica predicts:**
1. **Citation-prediction accuracy will scale smoothly and steeply with model size, and this is the arm where the effect should be strongest.** Galactica's 7%→52% (PWC) / 7%→37% (Contextual) sweep over 125M→120B says the raw *ability to name the right cited work* is highly scale-sensitive. Our arXiv arm inherits this: the linking behavior itself (does the model emit a resolvable citation?) should improve fast with scale, independent of the attention grant.
2. **Our attention edge should help most exactly where Galactica is weakest: the long tail and fine-grained content.** Galactica beats retrievers on aggregate accuracy but retains popular-paper bias and warns it needs augmentation for "fine-grained knowledge." Our read-access edge supplies the cited paper's *actual tokens*, so cross-doc reasoning that needs the target's specific content (not just its identity) is where our grant should beat a Galactica-style memorization baseline — and where a matched-compute `doc_concatenated`/`doc_concat_link` control (`masks.md`) should still fall short of `cross_doc_link` if the *directed link-position gating* matters. This is the ablation to run: our method vs. a "predict-the-citation-string-only" head with no grant, on a content-dependent metric (Δnll on the cited paper's continuation), not just link-string accuracy.
3. **Small-scale hallucination warning.** Galactica's title identifiers were "more prone to hallucination error at lower scales." Our models are far smaller than 30B/120B. Expect our generated links at small scale to frequently *fail to resolve* (miss the exact identifier). Two mitigations we already have that Galactica lacked: fuzzy title resolution (softens near-miss strings) and `corpus_then_generate` fallback (synthesizes the target when resolution misses). The reviewer-facing point: our resolution layer *decouples* link-string accuracy from retrieval success in a way Galactica's exact-match scoring cannot.
4. **Repeated-token headroom.** "Repeated Tokens Considered Not Harmful" (val loss falling through 4 epochs on a curated corpus, overfit only at epoch 5 for 120B) predicts that our curated graph corpora (unarXive, wiki, Stack) can be trained multi-epoch without the usual dedup-driven single-epoch caution — relevant because our corpora are smaller than web-scale. Caveat below.

**Open questions the two designs pose to each other:**
- *Galactica → us:* Does giving the model read-access to the target (our grant) reduce the popular-paper bias Galactica couldn't remove at 120B? Our exact resolution suggests yes for *retrieval fidelity*, but does the *decision of which link to emit* still inherit a frequency prior from the LM head? Worth measuring the frequency distribution of our *generated* (pre-resolution) links à la their KS-distance analysis.
- *Us → Galactica:* Galactica leaves "retrieval augmentation for future work." Our train/inference-unified insertion mechanism is a concrete answer to their open question — retrieval that is not a separate module but the *same* cross-doc mask realized at inference. We can position ours as the retrieval-augmented Galactica they gestured at, but with the augmentation trained end-to-end into the attention pattern rather than bolted on.

### Gotchas

- **Title-vs-ID identifier choice is a real fork, and it burned them at small scale.** They picked titles for accuracy but ate hallucination. Our node keys are hashed canonical identifiers, but our *detected link text* for arXiv is `\cite{title}` after rewriting (`data_pipelines.md`: unarXive titles, collisions disambiguated with `" (arxiv:id)"`). At small scale expect exactly Galactica's failure — the model emits a plausible-but-wrong title. Our fuzzy `HashNormTitleIndex` is the safety net they didn't have; make sure eval reports resolution rate separately from downstream Δnll so a hallucinated-but-fuzzily-resolved link isn't scored as a clean hit.
- **Popularity bias contaminates aggregate accuracy.** Galactica's high aggregate numbers partly reflect over-predicting famous papers. If we report our citation arm as a single accuracy number we inherit the same artifact. Stratify by target citation frequency (their KS analysis is the template) so the long-tail win from our read-access edge is visible.
- **Repeated-tokens finding is corpus-quality-conditional.** They stress it held for a *curated* corpus and suspected a quality/modality factor; do not read it as a blanket "epochs are free." Our Stack dedup is weak/sampling-only and wiki/arXiv have *no* dedup (`data_pipelines.md` reviewer-attackable #4). Multi-epoch training on our less-aggressively-deduped corpora risks memorizing near-duplicates — which for *citation prediction* would directly inflate the exact-match metric via train/test leakage. This is the sharper version of the contamination concern already in our notes.
- **2048 context is why they couldn't do what we do.** Any comparison must note the setups aren't apples-to-apples: Galactica physically could not attend into cited documents. Frame our contribution as enabled by the 32k packing + sparse grant, not as "we beat Galactica at their game."
- **Their retriever baselines are weak-ish.** ElasticSearch and a Contriever fine-tuned on only 10M pairs. Don't over-cite "LM beats retrieval" as settled; a stronger modern retriever narrows the gap. Our claim should rest on the *mechanism* (trained in-context edge) not on the retrieval-vs-parametric horse race.

## zhao2024analysing — Analysing the Impact of Sequence Composition on Language Model Pre-training

*(Zhao, Qu, Staniszewski, Tworkowski, Liu, Miłoś, Wu, Minervini — ACL 2024 long paper; arXiv 2402.13991. DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.427.)*

Sources: numbers below are from the ar5iv full-text render of 2402.13991 (tables 1–10) and the arXiv/ACL abstract. Where I could not confirm a figure against the full text I mark it *(inferred)*. Codebase comparisons cite our briefs `masks.md` and `packing_density.md` and the sources they name.

### What the paper actually does

The paper attacks the near-universal pretraining default: concatenate randomly-shuffled documents into fixed-length sequences and train with **full causal masking across the concatenation**, so a token can attend back into unrelated documents that happen to precede it in the pack. They argue (and measure) that this cross-document attention injects *distracting information* that hurts both LM loss and downstream tasks. Two orthogonal interventions:

1. **IntraDoc (intra-document causal masking).** Each token's likelihood is conditioned only on prior tokens *within its own document*; attention never crosses a document boundary in the pack. Implemented with FlashAttention-2's variable-length path (`flash_attn_varlen_qkvpacked_func` + `cu_seqlens` document-boundary offsets, `max_seqlen`, `causal=True`). Crucially they state IntraDoc "uses the same position information that is used in causal masking for each document" — i.e. **RoPE positions run per-document as if each document started fresh** (position reset at boundaries). Measured cost: **~4% pretraining slowdown** vs plain causal on 128×A100-80G. They note two naive alternatives are far worse: additive attention-bias masking is ~32% slower in xFormers and ~52% slower in vanilla PyTorch — the varlen kernel is what makes IntraDoc cheap.

2. **Sequence-construction methods** (what goes *into* a pack), three variants:
   - **MixChunk** (baseline): documents drawn uniformly at random from the whole corpus 𝒟; a single 2K/8K chunk can mix Wikipedia + GitHub + arXiv.
   - **UniChunk**: documents drawn uniformly from a *single source* 𝒟ₛ per chunk (no cross-domain mixing); batches sampled proportional to per-corpus token counts.
   - **BM25Chunk**: builds a chunk by *multi-hop BM25 retrieval* inside a per-source document buffer ℬₛ. Seed doc d₁ ~ Uniform(ℬₛ), then dᵢ₊₁ = Retrieve(dᵢ, ℬₛ); buffer refilled after each chunk. Query = the current doc with stopwords stripped and ≤ q=500 tokens sampled → O(q·k) retrieval. Docs concatenated with `[eos]` separators, last doc truncated to length L. BM25 config: buffer k=3072, index 244 GB, ~50K tok/s on 16 CPU cores.

**Training setup.** LLaMA-architecture decoder, "minor modifications to support intra-document masking." Main models **1.3B** params (24 layers, hidden 2048, 16 heads); ablations at **0.3B**. Data: **SlimPajama** (7 subcorpora: CommonCrawl, C4, Wikipedia, GitHub, StackExchange, arXiv, Book), **150B tokens** sampled. Context lengths **2K and 8K** studied side by side. 4M-token batch, 38,400 steps, ~40h on 128×A100-80G; AdamW (β=0.9/0.95, wd 0.1), cosine 3e-4 → 3e-5. Masking/construction affect only *pretraining*; downstream eval uses ordinary inference on the trained checkpoints.

**Headline numbers.**
- Perplexity (SlimPajama test, avg): 2K MixChunk **9.172** → BM25Chunk **8.550** (↓0.622) → IntraDoc **8.410** (↓0.883). 8K MixChunk 9.065 → BM25Chunk 8.289 → IntraDoc **8.079** (↓0.986). GitHub subcorpus moves most (IntraDoc 2K GitHub 4.252 vs Mix 5.531).
- In-context learning (avg acc): 2K 63.54 → BM25 **70.91** / IntraDoc 70.52; 8K 62.43 → IntraDoc **71.23** / BM25 69.47. Abstract's "+11.6%" = BM25 vs Mix at 2K.
- Knowledge memorisation / closed-book QA (EM, NQ+TQA avg): 2K Mix 10.33 → IntraDoc **11.60** (+12.3%); 8K Mix 7.99 → IntraDoc **10.99** (+37.5%). BM25 +9.8% at 2K.
- Reading comprehension + RAG (avg over RACE-h/m, SQuAD, HotpotQA, NQ-open, TQA-open): 2K Mix 30.31 → IntraDoc **32.49** / BM25 32.42; 8K Mix 29.60 → BM25 **34.54** / IntraDoc 33.18. Abstract's "+7.2%" context-utilisation = BM25.
- **Attention-distraction analysis (§5.1):** they define DistrProp = attention mass a token in doc 2 puts on the *irrelevant* doc 1. Under causal masking it stays **45–52%** on average even out to the 256th token of the second document — direct evidence that cross-doc attention is not benignly ignored. IntraDoc drives it to ~0; BM25Chunk lowers it because neighbours are related.
- **Burstiness (Zipf α, Table 5):** related-doc packing lowers α (more bursty); but IntraDoc beats UniChunk at *identical* α, so they conclude the causal-masking gains come from *reduced distraction*, not burstiness alone.
- Ablation (0.3B, 13B tok, valid PPL): Mix 15.474, IntraDoc 12.443, BM25 buffer-4096 12.528 (best buffer); w/o multi-hop 13.497; w/o retrieval (=UniChunk) 14.241; dense ContrieverChunk 13.720 (worse than BM25). A separator finding: causal models want `[eos]` between docs, but **IntraDoc prefers `\n`** because it never sees a mid-sequence `[eos]` in training.

### Methodology: theirs vs. ours

**The shared premise, opposite conclusions.** Both papers agree cross-document attention under naive random packing is harmful — their DistrProp 45–52% is exactly the "distraction" our block-diagonal `doc_causal` baseline is designed to exclude. Where they *ban* all cross-document attention (IntraDoc), we **selectively re-enable it along explicit link edges only** (`cross_doc_link`; `masks.md` §Formal semantics). Their result is our motivating null hypothesis: if uncontrolled cross-doc attention is net-negative, any cross-doc win we report must come from *edge-gated* attention being qualitatively different from the *random-neighbour* attention they measured.

**Axis 1 — train-on-structure vs retrieve-at-inference.** Neither paper retrieves at inference; both are pure pretraining-composition interventions. But the *structure signal* differs. BM25Chunk uses lexical-similarity retrieval to decide *co-packing* (which docs share a sequence); it then reads them under ordinary causal attention (BM25Chunk is a causal model — see Table 5). This is essentially the shi2024incontext / staniszewski2025structured lineage: **relatedness → adjacency, attention still uniform**. We go one step further: our edges are *given by the graph* (hyperlink/import/citation), and we add a **mask that grants read-access along the edge** (`cross_doc_link` grant rectangle rows [link_end_pos, A.end) × cols [B.start,B.end), `masks.md`:15). So BM25Chunk changes *what is adjacent*; we change *what may attend to what*, along a typed edge, at a specific token position.

**Axis 2 — attention-edge vs the alternatives.** Their IntraDoc is the pure "no edge" endpoint; BM25Chunk is "adjacency edge, no attention edge." Ours is an **asymmetric, position-gated attention edge**: only from the linking token onward (`link_end_pos`), only into the target doc, only backward in packing order (DAG gate, `cross_doc_mask.py:417-423`), never a symmetric GNN-style message-pass and never a cached-KV retrieval memory. This is a strictly different object from anything they test.

**Axis 3 — position handling is where we most diverge and are most exposed.** IntraDoc **resets RoPE per document** — each doc attends within itself at its own [0,len) positions, exactly as it would unpacked. Our `masks.md` reviewer-attackable #1 flags that we do **NOT** reset RoPE per doc: positions run global [0,T), so a linking doc A reads target B at a relative offset equal to the *packing distance*, which is traversal-order-dependent and semantically arbitrary. Zhao et al. deliberately avoided precisely this. This is the single sharpest methodological contrast, and their design is a direct argument that we should at least ablate per-doc position reset (or per-edge relative reset) for the granted rectangle.

**What we share.** (a) Both live on FlashAttention-family varlen/blocked kernels — their `flash_attn_varlen` for boundary masking is the moral ancestor of our `varlen_bim_v2` doc-causal kernel (`masks.md`:28, auto-select `doc_causal → varlen_bim_v2`). (b) Both treat *related-document co-packing* as a lever: their BM25Chunk buffer is a lexical analog of our graph-traversal packer (`traversal.md` / `packing_density.md` `prefer_targets_first` Kahn topo sort). (c) Both study 2K vs long context and find long-context gains concentrate in retrieval/RAG tasks.

**What our design resolves that they left open.** Their paper cannot separate "related neighbours help because of *burstiness/curriculum*" from "…because cross-doc attention *would* help if only it were to the right document." IntraDoc kills all cross-doc attention, so it can't test the second hypothesis; BM25Chunk keeps cross-doc attention but only to lexically-similar neighbours and doesn't isolate the attention channel. **Our compute-control ladder does exactly this isolation**: `doc_causal < cross_doc_link ≤ doc_concat_link ≤ doc_concatenated` (`masks.md`:16-18). `doc_concat_link` (whole-doc grant) is a strict FLOP-superset of `cross_doc_link` with identical connectivity, isolating the *link-position gating*; `doc_concatenated` gives maximal cross-doc attention with *no* link structure. If `cross_doc_link` beats `doc_concatenated` at matched FLOPs, we've shown the win is the *typed edge*, not raw cross-doc attention — the exact question their DistrProp analysis raises but cannot answer.

### Predictions & open questions for our method

- **Baseline direction confirmed.** Our plain-causal-over-a-pack condition (if we ever run one, i.e. `doc_concatenated` with no component structure, or a fully-mixed pack) should *lose* to `doc_causal`, reproducing their IntraDoc > MixChunk. If it doesn't, suspect a data or eval bug before believing cross-doc attention is free.
- **Effect should be strongest on GitHub / code and on long context.** Their largest PPL wins are GitHub (2K 5.531 → 4.252) and their long-context (8K) gains dominate on RAG/closed-book. Our import-edge (code) traversal on TheStack is the analog; predict `cross_doc_link`'s clearest win is on code with real import edges, and grows with T (we train at 32k, far past their 8K — the distraction problem they measure gets *worse* with length, so our edge-gating should matter more, not less).
- **DistrProp gives us a diagnostic.** We can compute their DistrProp metric on `doc_concatenated` (should be high, 45–52%-like) vs `cross_doc_link` (should concentrate mass on the *linked* target, near-zero on unlinked docs). This is a cheap, publishable intermediate result that would directly show our mask converts harmful diffuse cross-doc attention into useful targeted attention.
- **Position-reset ablation is likely necessary.** Given IntraDoc's care with per-doc positions, predict our no-RoPE-reset choice (`masks.md` attackable #1) is a *live confound*: some of any `cross_doc_link` win/loss could be RoPE-offset noise. Ablation to run: per-doc position reset with the granted rectangle re-based to a fixed relative offset. If `cross_doc_link` only wins with reset positions, that reframes our contribution.
- **BM25/related-packing is complementary, not competing.** BM25Chunk (their retrieval packer) improves *causal* models; our graph packer already co-locates linked docs (`prefer_targets_first`). Prediction: combining related-packing (which we do) with the attention edge (which they don't) should be additive — their BM25Chunk gain (~+11% ICL) is the *packing* portion; our edge should add on top. This is exactly the decomposition our compute controls are built to measure.
- **Open question we may answer for them.** They speculate burstiness correlates with performance but find IntraDoc breaks the correlation (beats UniChunk at equal α). Our typed edges let us vary connectivity while holding co-packing fixed — a cleaner instrument to test whether it's *distraction reduction* or *distribution shape* that drives gains.

### Gotchas

- **Separator token interacts with the mask.** Their sharpest tuning trap: causal models want `[eos]` between docs, but IntraDoc trains best with `\n` because it never sees a mid-sequence `[eos]`. We use a single EOS suffix, no BOS (`packing_density.md`:27; `masks.md` attackable #2: `-1`/EOS layout positions form one pseudo-doc that attends itself). Because `cross_doc_link` *does* let attention cross boundaries along grants, our separator handling sits between their two regimes — watch that the EOS/layout-gap semantics don't silently leak attention (their finding says the separator choice is worth an explicit ablation, not a default).
- **DistrProp is measured, distraction is real, not a modeling artifact.** 45–52% attention mass on the irrelevant doc even 256 tokens in — do not assume the model "learns to ignore" preceding docs. If we ever relax the mask for a compute control, expect real degradation, not a no-op.
- **Position IDs across packed docs.** Their explicit per-doc position reset is a warning shot at our global-position choice; if our long-context (32k) numbers look noisy or traversal-order-sensitive, RoPE-offset is a prime suspect (packing distance varies run-to-run).
- **Contamination via retrieval packing.** BM25Chunk retrieves within a source buffer; if our graph traversal packs a doc and its near-duplicate/linked-back copy into the same 32k window, eval leakage and inflated cross-doc "gains" follow. Their distinct-n-gram table (IntraDoc distinct-2gram 80.35 vs Mix 71.84) shows retrieval packing *raises* within-sequence duplication — our link traversal will too. Dedup and check distinct-n-gram before trusting a `cross_doc_link` win.
- **Long-context eval is where effects concentrate but also where variance lives.** Their RAG/MDQA (lost-in-the-middle) gains are the largest but on small-EM-scale tasks (HotpotQA EM 7–14). Small denominators → noisy deltas; use enough eval items.
- **Efficiency accounting.** IntraDoc is only +4% *with the right kernel* and +32–52% with a naive additive-bias mask. Our `masks.md`:23 bit-packed-grant / pointwise-membership trick is our analog of "don't build the dense mask" — their numbers are a concrete reminder that a naive dense `cross_doc_link` mask (~1GB, O(T²)) is a non-starter and the kernel is load-bearing for the *claim*, not just speed.

\paragraph{Contrasts and controls.}
Each cross-document benchmark scores the same target tokens under two conditions:
\crossdoc attention with the granted linked document(s) present, versus the flat
\doccausal baseline over the identical context and completion (the cross-vs-flat
$\Delta$nll). Two further controls isolate the effect. The \emph{placebo} (derangement)
re-scores each fired query with the granted document's content replaced by a different
example's while holding the link identifiers fixed, so the grant still fires but the model
attends to a wrong-but-plausible document; the placebo separation (placebo minus
cross-document NLL) measures specificity to the correct link. To rule out verbatim
re-exposure of duplicated text, we additionally stratify the $\Delta$nll by the
target--context $n$-gram overlap $\alpha$ (RETRO's leakage protocol) and report the effect
within the lowest-overlap stratum. Confidence intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap over
the paired per-example deltas.

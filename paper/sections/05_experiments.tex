\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Models and budget.}
All reported models share one architecture: $1024$-dimensional, $24$ layers, $8$ heads
(head dimension $128$), $32{,}768$-token context, GPT-2 vocabulary, roughly $350$M
parameters with the optional value-embedding banks disabled (\Cref{app:recipe}). A
training step is $8$ packs of $32$k tokens ($262{,}144$ tokens per optimizer step,
one pack per GPU, no gradient accumulation) on A100-80GB nodes under SLURM. The
single-source runs train for about $15$k steps ($\approx3.9$B tokens); on the
Wikipedia graph this is four passes over the training split, each pass a distinct
traversal (a different seed re-packs every document with a different subset and
order of its neighbours), so repeated epochs re-sample the co-occurrence structure
rather than replaying it.

\paragraph{Recipe.}
The optimizer and schedule are inherited (\Cref{app:recipe}) but two hyperparameters
were retuned for this data regime because the inherited values, set for a short
FineWeb speedrun, were badly off: an eight-point Muon learning-rate sweep on the
\doccausal Wikipedia model found a clean U-curve with its minimum at
$\mu_\text{lr}=0.003$ (the inherited $0.001$ was second-worst), and a weight-decay
sweep found the inherited $1.2$ roughly an order of magnitude too high, with the
minimum at $0.1$. AdamW's rate is coupled at $0.01\times$ Muon's. Unless stated
otherwise every run uses $\mu_\text{lr}=0.003$, warmup $300$ steps, a linear cooldown
over the final $40\%$ to $0.1\times$ peak, the embedding/head untie at two-thirds of
training, and $\texttt{max\_grants}=256$. The weight decay of each arm is listed with
its result (\Cref{app:results}); within a paired contrast both arms share every
hyperparameter, so the within-model $\Delta$ is robust to the exact operating point.

\paragraph{The cross-document contrast.}
Our headline metric is a paired, same-token contrast. For a benchmark item with a
context $c$, a completion $y$, and a document $B$ that $c$ links to, we score the
teacher-forced negative log-likelihood of $y$ twice on the \emph{same} model: once
with $B$ packed before $c$ and the \crossdoc grant open from the link in $c$ into $B$
(\emph{cross-doc}), and once with the identical $c$ and $y$ under \doccausal
(\emph{flat}), where $B$ is either absent from the sequence (RepoBench) or packed but
unreadable (HotpotQA) --- equivalent for the scored tokens under document-isolated
attention, up to positional offset. We report
$\Delta\mathrm{nll}=\mathrm{nll}_\text{flat}-\mathrm{nll}_\text{cross}$ over the
subset of items on which the link detector fires and resolves (the \emph{fired}
subset, of size $n$), with bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals. Because both arms
score the same tokens on the same weights, memorization of $y$ cancels: the contrast
measures only what attending into $B$ through the link adds.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{HotpotQA cross-doc} (Wikipedia). Bridge-type questions from HotpotQA
        \citep{yang2018hotpotqa}: the second supporting article $B$ is the target, the
        first supporting article $A$---with its hyperlinks rewritten to Markdown
        \texttt{[text](Title)} form---is the context, followed by the question and
        \texttt{Answer:}; the completion is the answer. Items are kept only if some
        sentence of $A$ links to $B$'s title, and two documented structural non-fires
        (parenthesized and quoted titles) are left as non-fires rather than forced.
  \item \textbf{RepoBench cross-doc} (Python, Java). From RepoBench
        \citep{liu2024repobench}: the item's cross-file snippets are the targets, the
        import statement plus the cropped in-file code is the context, and the next
        line is the completion. Each import path is matched precisely to its snippet
        (Java imports are resolved to source-root-relative fully-qualified names).
  \item \textbf{Internal cross-file ports} (the other code languages). For languages
        with no external cross-file benchmark we build the same item shape from our own
        held-out community splits and score at the \emph{use line}---the first statement
        that references a symbol declared in the granted file, located with a
        tree-sitter declaration query---which removes import-syntax artefacts and
        concentrates the signal where the dependency is used. These carry a
        \emph{placebo} arm: the same identifiers (so grants still fire) with the target
        content swapped for another item's, testing whether the gain comes from the
        \emph{right} document or from any extra in-language tokens. The headline
        HotpotQA and RepoBench arms do not yet carry this placebo
        (\Cref{sec:analysis}).
  \item \textbf{Community-pack perplexity.} Packs built by BFS over a held-out
        \texttt{val\_community} split (intact linked subgraphs, \Cref{app:datasets}) are
        scored under \crossdoc versus \doccausal on the same model, grants taken
        directly from graph edges; $\Delta\mathrm{nll}$ is over documents with at least
        one incoming grant. This is the metric of the density experiments below.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Single-document controls.}
Isolated-document held-out perplexity and six likelihood-scored multiple-choice
benchmarks---HellaSwag \citep{zellers2019hellaswag}, ARC-easy \citep{clark2018arc},
PIQA \citep{bisk2020piqa}, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, and BoolQ---scored under
\doccausal for every model. No link can fire on an isolated document, so these
measure whether a mask condition changes the base language model at all.

\paragraph{Ablation designs.}
\emph{Four-mask}: \doccausal, \crossdoc, \concatlink, \concat on the Wikipedia graph
with identical data order and recipe. \emph{Traversal}: $\{$bfs, dfs, random walk,
random$\}\times\{\doccausal,\crossdoc\}$ on Wikipedia (HotpotQA cross-doc) and on Java
(RepoBench cross-doc). \emph{Link density}: with the corpus, packing, and model fixed,
a seeded per-edge subsample keeps a fraction $\kappa\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1\}$ of the
resolved grants; $\kappa=0$ is exactly \doccausal, which anchors the instrument. The
subsample is applied either at evaluation only (one trained \crossdoc checkpoint, how
much the model \emph{uses} density) or at training (retrain per $\kappa$, then
evaluate at every $\kappa$, how much density helps \emph{learning}), giving a
train-$\kappa\times$eval-$\kappa$ grid per dataset; the honest cross-dataset density
axis is the number of grants the mask actually realizes per pack, not raw graph
out-degree, since edges whose targets are not co-packed can never be attended.
\emph{Diversity}: one model trained jointly on the eleven linked sources versus the
single-source specialists, as a \crossdoc/\doccausal pair per token budget; its
results await a corrected re-run (\Cref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Compute controls}
Granting the baselines more within-context attention does not close the gap. On code
RepoBench perplexity, \crossdoc reaches \val{compute.repobench_ppl.cross_doc_link}, whereas
\concatlink and \concat --- which see the same concatenated multi-document context --- sit at
\val{compute.repobench_ppl.doc_concat_link} and \val{compute.repobench_ppl.doc_concatenated}
respectively, and the plain \doccausal baseline at \val{compute.repobench_ppl.doc_causal}.
The compute controls remain well above \crossdoc, so the gain comes from the linking
inductive bias, not from additional attention FLOPs.

\subsection{Traversal ablation}
Graph-structured packing matters for the base language model independent of the
cross-document objective: final validation loss is \val{traversal.wiki_val_loss.graph}
under graph (bfs/dfs) packing versus \val{traversal.wiki_val_loss.random} under random
packing. The incremental cross-document benefit is itself traversal-robust --- the Java
cross-document $\Delta$nll above is positive and similar across bfs, dfs, and random-walk
traversals --- so packing quality and cross-document attention are two decoupled axes.
